## The mountain that rivers climb

Reverse Mountain is not sailed *to* so much as *up*. Cold deep currents from the East, West, North and South Blue slam into the Red Line here and, with nowhere else to go, run uphill through four narrow canals. Miss the mouth of the canal by a ship's width and the hull meets rock instead of water.

At the summit the four streams merge and drop down a fifth canal — the one exit, and the first stretch of water that truly belongs to the Grand Line.

### Navigator's notes

- Enter dead centre. The current does the climbing; the helm only has to hold the line.
- Ordinary compasses spin uselessly from the summit onward. This is where a crew first learns it needs a **Log Pose**.
- Seven magnetic routes fan out from the foot of the mountain. Whichever island the needle records first decides the whole voyage.

### At the foot of the mountain

Twin Capes guards the exit, along with its lighthouse keeper Crocus — and Laboon, an island whale the size of a mountain who has spent fifty years ramming the Red Line, waiting for a crew that promised to come back.

> A promise made at the entrance to the sea is the kind you carry all the way around the world.
